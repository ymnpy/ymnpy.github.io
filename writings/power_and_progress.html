power and progress

I have finished reading a book called Power and Progress (Daron Acemoglu & Simon Johnson) second time. I have checked my writings and didn't see any mention about this book, so lets try to give a brief overview about it.

I have read this book a second time, because its so relevant to our time. Many time it criticizes "Technology is always good, it brings prosper to everyone in the end." kind of sayings which we often hear from some techno-optimists or some AI company CEO. But authors in this book, with concrete examples from the past show us that this is not the case. Technology is a moldable thing. If you mold it wrongly, it may bring disaster to humans. For instance: very famously in the first half of the Industrial Revolution say between 1790-1850, new machines like steam engines and their derivatives like textile machines did not make lives of England working class better, it actually made things worse. People had to work longer hours because employers wanted to maximize their profit (welcome to capitalism) so to catch up higher production rates they did not employ more workers, instead they made existing workers work harder and longer. People were crammed up into cities which had no running water or toilet system. People were throwing their feces on to the streets they wrote, so imagine the situation, imagine the ilness rates. Yes, technology brought higher production rates in this case but it also brought disaster. Examples can be multiplied (wind mills in feudal age for instance.) Authors make a very definitive distinction here and say higher production rates does not equal to higher prosperity for all. Because higher production rates can increase the output but what matters is marginal production rate, from my understanding, means that how much output is produced by one worker. Solely production rate does not care about human, its not even in the equation, but latter one does. So once human is disposable they had to work harder, and bear with all the bad conditions or they would starve basically. But increasing worker production itself, like giving him a calculator, or giving a designer CAD program, increase their production. They become valuable to use these tools and under fair free market these workers are looked after by companies so their wage increase etc.

So once authors break the narrative of tech is always good, they give us some ideas about how we can avoid such disasters with AI. Because they also emphasize that most if technological developments focus on automation mostly or data accumulation (its like money accumulation for a capitalist if you think about, an interesting parallel in the modern age, data = money !?) they dont benefit to the society. And they say that is the direction we are running towards these days (the book is written in 2023). Additionally the also claim that current AI status does not really increase production yet they increase unemployment. So it does not give and net positive output, yet put some negativity into the equation by making people lay off from their job and not making any new jobs (maybe few but not enough). When you think about it it kinda make sense, no ? Which product has been produced at significant higher rates since 2022 GPT-3 came up for instance ? Maybe software ? But does it really touch our lives directly like food, cars, medicine etc ? I don't think so. So to avoid such directions, and mold the technology progress as we said before we, as a society can do things. Technology progress is not an arrow that once you shoot, you don't have any control over it and it just goes, no it is not that.

Things we can do they summarize in three steps basically:

  • Change the narrative: The current narrative is owned by techno-optimist, techno-oligarchs. We need to stop believing everything these guys says. They have money and popularity and use these to shape technological progress in a way that benefits them not society in general. We need to question their decisions and manifest our demands. We need to re-wire our brain around this subject in a way.

  • Make opposing forces: Following the previous point, we need some balancing or opposing forces like strong civil societal organizations. In the second phase of the industrial revolution, after 1850s we see improvements in working class people's lives. Because they had demanding worker unions. Authors emhphasize many times importance of such unions against the status-quo. We need to push our demands on those people by making unions, civil societal organizations, by raising our voices in the streets etc.

  • Political applications: Lastly, making our voice heard and our demands not rejectable we may expect good regulations around AI and automation. For instance goverments may put some technological goal for companies to compete which benefits society. They can help monetarily to those companies who does not choose robots over humans and punish monetarily (which I guess capitalists hate and try to avoid) do the reverse so in a way human labor may still be attractive. They can also support academics instead of letting them be influenced by corporations and make biased publications/researchs which benefits to those corporations. I guess examples can also be added in this matter as well.

The above points are authors' solutions to the problem, briefly, according to my understanding. Agree or not to what they wrote (I don't agree with everything they say, they are more likely biased towards reforms but I am more biased towards revolution, I believe the system is rigged and we need to get rid of it instead trying to fix it.) there are some valuable points or thought provoking ideas in them. We can all feel the influence of those techno-oligarchs over the society and wonder how the technology they are building is going to benefit us all. We need to open our eyes not and let those people run our lives with their narrative and influence. Because past events does not always support their claims. As a machine learning model does, we may use those examples from the past as a good data source and make predictions about the future. And if we do, we may predict a dangerous future if we train our models well and not let them make us overfit to their narratives.
