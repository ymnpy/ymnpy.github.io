wealth of nations according to Smith

I have finished reading the reading a relatively famous book called Wealth of Nations by Adam Smith (I feel like I have done this introduction to my writing many times so maybe I should try something different next time). For the record, it took me around 1 month to read it and I was pretty consistent about reading it everyday which I do with my books anyway. So I want to write about some important topics I came across in the book to make this journey more persistent in my mind inshaAllah.

I want to start with the title of the book, Wealth of Nations, and what Adam Smith meant by that (as a side note I also want to add that the man, Adam Smith, himself is a pretty unique character, probably a very intellectual person and it is said that he would talk to himself or smile at things that noone was seeing occasionally and I remember a video named him as -absent minded professor-). Adam Smith, basically saying that wealth of a nation is not equal to accumulation of money or gold/silver in that time. By saying that he is criticising the dominant economical model of his time called mercantalism. These people were basically arguing that just like a man who has good amount of money in his pocket is called wealthy, a nation which has gold/silver or money is also called wealthy as well. To do so they are defending to increase export and decrease import by governmental actions like tariffs etc. In their model government has huge role and most of the time end up giving advantage to their domestic merchants by giving them a monopoly over a good. Because since there is no import, the competition is almost non-existent (they can also gather and make a cartel for inside market so imagine the rest). So, Adam Smith is starting by saying the wealth, or more specifically a value of a good is not about money, money is just a transaction meta, its about how much labor put into it. So a wealthy person is called who has ability to move or control big amount of labor. And since labor eventually is used to produce a good, we can change the previous sentence as, wealthy person is called who has ability to move or control big amount of goods. So for a nation this becomes, a wealthy nation is called which posses many produced goods in its hand. Basically todays GPD, or it reminds me of it. Because say you are producing 1000 cars a year as a nation and your neighbor only produce 100 cars, you are wealthier than him. Because eventhough you don't need 1000 cars and only need 200 cars lets say in your domestic market, you can always trade the remaining 800 cars and import many goods to your country so your people can enjoy them. That's basically wealth for Adam Smith, and I must say its pretty logical. Today its not about the money in our pockets because inflation as you can imagine is destroying it, its about how much labor or equally, how much goods we can purchase with it, that is the important nuance. Money is just a piece of paper backed by government.

Once Adam Smith establish this argument he then gives some advices about how to produce more things. I guess it all come down to increasing production efficiency. Because with decreasing effort if you produce more, then you can produce many things quantitavely. This will drop the prices in your country's market so people can buy from these things easily. And since your efficiency is increasing you will produce more and require more labour which will also increase employment and employment wages, another plus for society as well. Also with your surplus products you can trade with other nations what you don't produce efficiently. So you as a nation should increase your efficiency, thats one thing. You can do that by division of labour or implementing machining to your production process etc. He explain these things in detail. Secondly, you should let things flow basically a.k.a. laissez-faire. Adam Smith kind of sees whole this process like a natural tendency, if you let market go it will arrange itself in the most efficient way and any restriction to change its direction is going to harm it in most cases. So you can see how this opinion contradicts with mercantalism.

He has many other important ideas and insights about the economics about society etc. In his fifth book (all wealth of nations consist of five books assembled together today, atleast thats what I got) he talks about objectives of a ruler, taxation, first professional armies, how education can be improved etc. This guy is beyond economics, he is more like a sociologist or philosopher with having masters in economics. He has interesting observations as well so not only theoretical, he probably have seen things in real life as well. One thing he observed famously is that, every person in the society look after for himself by producing a good and selling it to the society. His number one priority is himself, is making money (you may call it selfish and I would probably disagree because this is not that filthy selfishness this is something else in my opinion, also by saying himself we can also add his family as well I guess) so he can what he needs by trading that money later. But by doing so he is also helping the society. He call this invisible hand, which means somehow a person who is thinking about himself economically is also, without realizing helping the society, an invisible hand aligning their goals. He use this phrase only once by the way, only once...

Another impression he left on me is that, he is probably misunderstood or some people mis-represent him intentionally. Because he does not fully claim hardcore-capitalism where you increase the efficiency, do your division labor and don't care about the consequences. He is very aware of that division of labor can decrease mental sharpness of workers. He is aware that capitalists (in my book capitalism is not mentioned even once but I can understand what he means by that) can gather and plot things against the society or they may work against the benefit of society etc. So he is, to an extent, aware of the dangers of capitalism. He is just putting the facts there, giving the formulas to increase production and why it is necessary than giving you warnings to be careful about the consequences. But he has also couple of contradictions or missing dots in his arguments. For instance, I did not quite understand -how new value is produced- Because in one chapter he criticizes physiocrats, a group of people that says wealth only comes from soil, through agriculture because you put 1 and take 3 for example. Adam Smith, after giving them some credits of course, criticizes this and says industry is also making value by changing the quality of things. But if you think about it this is just transformation or synhetising of materials nothing new is produced, quantitiy of things is not increased. Yes there is value in it, I wont deny that, but from the impression I took from the book can't see how yet. Maybe the labor of workers are not attached to it thats why. Also at first, he claims that value of all products is labor but then he is also make another explanation about all wealth is connected to grain since its value is not changing dramatically throughout the years. So is it labor or grain, or it is labor but to measure labor of two things relatively, we need another reference point as grain ? I am not really sure about these points, these are some missing points for me. So maybe I couldn't understand those statements or maybe he really left behind some gaps which is quite possible since he is just a human.

Anyways, I think Adam Smith is a quite unique person as I mentioned it before and he is today a bit misrepresented in my opinion. So whoever is interested in about him or in economics will find abundance of information in his book but it is not an easy ready I must warn. Most of the time he is given the title of -father of classical economics-, I can see why (division of labor, increasing efficiency, free market etc.) but recently I also have heard that Ibn Khaldun talked about similar things as well. So not quite sure about whether he deserve that title or not. I also have intention about reading Mukaddime by Ibn Khaldun so I will see the answer of this question myself inshaAllah.
