precious autonomy in gaming

Maybe the thing that connected us to games was autonomy.

Autonomy is one of the things that is greatly lacking in our lives today.

Our lives revolve around the places we have to go, the spaces we are expected to be in, and the requirement to be there at a certain time.

We can't see our families. We can't meet up with our friends. We can't make time for our hobbies.

We have to sit in an office from 9 to 5, stand in a workshop, or sit in a classroom staring at a board.

Sometimes, perhaps most of the time, we're not really doing anything meaningful.

We're simply playing the role assigned to us.

A role that was defined for us... by others.

In games, however, we had autonomy. In RPGs, perhaps even more so.

We were whoever we wanted to be.

We had whatever traits we wanted.

We could be wherever we wanted, whenever we wanted. We could take on whatever quest we wanted, or simply chase after pointless things, like hunting for a mount or whatever.

But the decision was ours.

We had autonomy.

Not everything I've described applies to every game, of course. But many of the games we considered successful did contain these elements.

Think of the games they call AAA games. The big RPGs. The massive MMORPGs.

The more autonomy and customization the player was given, the more enjoyment we seemed to get from the game.

Customization.

Maybe that's the word we need to focus on.

In games, we were individuals. Separate and unique people.

In the modern world, on the other hand, we seem to have become people stripped of these things—people stripped of autonomy and individuality.

In games, some of us had long hair, while others had short hair.

Some dressed their characters in flashy, elaborate gear, while others didn't put any enhancements on their equipment at all.

Some invested their points into Skill A, while others strengthened Skill B.

These decisions belonged to us.

And the results were remarkably diverse and colorful.

It was as if all of this was filling something we had lost in this age, and that was what drew us into these worlds.

Autonomy.

Maybe that's why we loved those games so much.

The decisions were ours.

We could be wherever we wanted, whenever we wanted. We could be whoever we wanted to be.

We weren't a bunch of radical anarchists running around causing harm to everyone.

We were simply a group of players who made their own choices—cooperative, happy, sometimes ambitious, and capable of enjoying the moment.

Our autonomy belonged to us.

And in a way, we were free.

I'm not saying everyone should go back to playing games, of course.

I'm just saying that maybe this was the thing that drew us to them in the first place.

And perhaps this is something that can be achieved in real life too.

But before that, maybe we need to recognize what it is that we've lost.
